# PnLCalib vs our homography on the bench (2026-10-02)

vb03 put stage 4 at 15% within 2 m: the roboflow pitch model put box-front keypoints on a mowing stripe, and the fit accepted them (vision bench review, clip 3). Before planning a PTZ solver (roadmap step b), a 30-minute look at whether an off-the-shelf SoccerNet calibration model does better. It does, by a lot.

## What was run
- **PnLCalib** (github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib, the follow-up to "No Bells, Just Whistles"). Two HRNetV2-W48 heatmap nets, one for keypoints and one for line extremities, at 960×540. A full camera (focal, position, rotation) is chosen by heuristic voting over the candidates, then refined on points and lines (`--pnl_refine`). Base single-view weights `SV_kp` / `SV_lines`, trained on SoccerNet-Calibration; not the WC14/TSWC finetunes. GPL-2.0.
- It's cloned outside the repo (`../PnLCalib`), with its two extra dependencies (shapely, lsq-ellipse) in a side folder. Torch and OpenCV come from the project venv; nothing was added to the project.
- `scripts/pnl_compare.py`: every 5th unmarked clip frame. The vision run's own player/keeper boxes (bottom center) go through PnLCalib's ground-plane homography (`P[:, [0, 1, 3]]`) and, separately, the pipeline's `pitch_x/pitch_y`. Both are matched to PFF the way `vision.bench` does it (Hungarian, 5 m gate, same sync). Misses and frames without a fit count as failures. Axes are picked by agreement with the pipeline, and come out the same on all three clips.
- Not the bench: no view gate, no temporal filter, every 5th frame only. The pipeline numbers are its cached run (vb01's run is on `circle_kp_x_m` 9.15), and they come within 1 pt of the bench's.

## Results

| Clip | PnLCalib within 2 m | median / p90 | Pipeline within 2 m | median / p90 | PnLCalib no-fit frames |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vb01 ARG–FRA | **81.7%** | 0.60 / 1.43 m | 14.8% | 2.73 / 4.40 m | 46 / 290 |
| vb02 NED–ARG | **91.2%** | 0.57 / 1.33 m | 32.5% | 2.24 / 3.95 m | 6 / 280 |
| vb03 JPN–ESP | **84.1%** | 0.45 / 0.98 m | 15.8% | 3.09 / 4.57 m | 35 / 360 |

- **Same foot points, same truth:** the gain is all in the camera. With a good camera, the foot anchor and PFF's own position error leave a median of about 0.5 m.
- **vb02 reaches 91%, but not on the bench's terms:** every 5th frame, no view gate, no temporal filter. The bench number with PnLCalib as stage 4 is still to come.
- **No-fit frames are the gap, and they're on clean wide play.** vb01 47/290, vb02 6, vb03 35/360. On vb03 the view gate passes everything and the pipeline fits ~98% of frames, so here PnLCalib is *worse* on availability than what it replaces. The failures come in runs, not scattered: vb03 257.5–260.5 s and 281–285 s, vb01 78–83.3 s, vb02 22.2–23.0 s. They're ordinary views: midfield between the box and the circle, a box view, and on vb02 even a frame with halfway and the circle in view. So holding the last fit won't cover a 3–5 s run. It needs a fallback (our fit, or a camera model carried forward), a lower voting threshold, or the WC14/TSWC finetunes. This is the first acceptance question for the 03 plan.
- vb03's frame at 250 s (the one where the pipeline's halfway line tilts and its circle sits high): PnLCalib's halfway line, circle and far touchline sit on the painted ones.
- **Speed on the RTX 2060:** about 420 ms/frame end to end. The two nets are 305 ms (fp32) or 185 ms (fp16 autocast); the voting and refinement add ~60 ms (refinement itself ~10 ms). Peak GPU memory is 1.9 GB.

## What it means
- **Stage 4's keypoint model is the problem, not the fit.** Swapping the calibration takes within 2 m from 15–33% to 82–91% on all three stadiums. A PTZ solver on top of the roboflow keypoints would be fighting the wrong input.
- **Plan in 03 next:** PnLCalib (or its nets with our acceptance logic) as stage 4. The open questions for that plan:
  - **Speed.** Offline, every frame costs ~0.3–0.4 s on the 2060, against ~0.25 s for the whole pipeline now. Per-frame isn't needed: the camera moves smoothly, so calibrating every few frames and holding or interpolating between them is likely enough. That's where a PTZ model could still help, as the temporal part (fixed position, pan/tilt/zoom smoothed). Live at 30 fps needs a fraction of frames, fp16/TensorRT, or a smaller backbone.
  - **Acceptance.** What counts as a rejected fit (PnLCalib returns none on 2–16% of frames), and how the view gate and the replay check work with a full camera instead of a homography.
  - **Replay and the cache.** The bench replays from the keypoint cache. The cache would hold PnLCalib's camera parameters (or its keypoints and lines), so threshold sweeps still run without the GPU.
  - **License, open.** PnLCalib is GPL-2.0, and `pnl_compare` imports it in-process. That's fine for an offline experiment; it's not settled for the pipeline or the public demo. The weights are trained on SoccerNet-Calibration, whose video is NDA-gated (06), and their terms aren't checked yet. The user decides before integration.
  - **WC14/TSWC finetunes:** untested here; worth a run since the bench is World Cup footage.
- The 31/32 `circle_kp_x_m` question goes away with the roboflow model.

## Sync check on PnLCalib positions
With a ~0.5 m median, the offset curve (`pnl_compare`, ±1 s in PFF-frame steps) has a sharp minimum, unlike the bench's flat `--offset-check` on the old geometry:

| Clip | Sync | Best offset vs the sync | Median at best |
|---|---|---|---|
| vb01 | cut edges | +0.033 s | 0.599 m |
| vb02 | cut edges | +0.000 s | 0.574 m |
| vb03 | kicks + 0.11 s | +0.067 s | 0.441 m |

- Both cut-edge syncs are right to a PFF frame. The "PFF lags 0.23 / 0.53 s" from the bench's `--offset-check` was the bad geometry.
- **The kick correction holds:** vb03 lands 2 PFF frames off. Without the +0.11 s it would have been 0.18 s off. No change to the manifest; the bench's ±1 s check can now use these numbers.
- The median rises ~0.15 m per 0.1 s either way, so sync errors of a frame or two don't matter at this level.
