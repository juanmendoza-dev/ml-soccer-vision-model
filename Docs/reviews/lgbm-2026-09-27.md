# LightGBM baseline on hand features (2026-09-27)

First real model through the CV path: `python -m prediction.cv --model lgbm` (`prediction/lgbm.py`, features in `prediction/features.py`, 29 of them). Labels from resample `b31a0eb`, same frozen folds and τ rule as the floor. The model code ran at `da9e620`; the run is stamped with the commit at save time (`3480dd0`, later commits only touched evaluation and docs). The generated report for the held-ball run is below the findings, unchanged.

## Before the model: PFF's ESTIMATED ball uses later frames
- 25.6% of scored rows have a ball PFF marks ESTIMATED. The spec PDF is unreadable, so this was measured on 13 games (8,422 ESTIMATED runs with a VISIBLE ball on both sides).
- The estimated path isn't a straight line between detections, but **it ends on the next detection**. For runs of ≥ 1 s where the ball moved > 5 m (2,261 runs), the last estimated position is 0.5 m from the next visible one (median, p90 1.4 m, 85% under 1 m). That doesn't grow with the gap: 0.4 m after 30 m, 3+ s gaps. A past-only estimate can't know where the ball reappears, so it leaks, and vision can't reproduce it live.
- So features use a **held ball** by default (`ball_source=held`): VISIBLE detections only, the last one carried forward up to 1 s (vision's `ball_max_gap_s`), with `ball_age_s`. 15.1% of scored rows then have no ball. Velocities are recomputed on the grid from visible positions only: 02's `vx`/`vy` difference over 0.2 s and would pull in an estimated position right after a gap. `ball_source=raw` keeps the ESTIMATED ball, only to measure the leak.
- Tests: every feature is unchanged when frames after t change; scrambling every ESTIMATED position (ball and players) changes no held feature; history is rotated with the anchor row's flip.

## Findings
- **LightGBM clearly beats the floor, on every fold.** H = 5: pooled PR-AUC **0.296** vs **0.177** for the floor on the same held ball (base rate 0.025), ROC-AUC 0.931 vs 0.901. Per fold 0.298 ± 0.023. It wins on **5/5 folds** for PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier and miss rate, at both horizons (`evaluation.compare`). H = 3: 0.298 vs 0.159.
- **Calibrated without a calibration step** (no class weights): the top decile predicts 0.186 and sees 0.185. Every other decile is within 0.003.
- **It can alarm now, just late.** At ≤ 3 false alarms per match (τ ≈ 0.57–0.64, picked on inner matches), it catches **174 of 1,154 open-play shots** (miss rate 0.849, was 0.990 for the floor) at 2.5 false alarms per match. But the median lead is **0.7 s** (q25–q75 0.37–0.97 s), and only 3 shots get ≥ 2 s. **00's ≥ 2 s lead is not met.**
- **Why the lead is short:** p rises steadily toward the shot, but not far. Median p over open-play shots: 0.05 at 5 s before, 0.16 at 2 s, 0.23 at 1 s, 0.30 at 0.2 s. Only 20% of shots have p > 0.3 two seconds out, and τ sits near 0.6. The τ sweep shows the trade: τ = 0.2 catches 78% of shots with a 1.6 s median lead, at 73 false alarms per match. Attacks that get near goal without a shot still look like attacks that end in one.
- **Budget transfer:** τ from the inner matches gives 1.5–3.9 false alarms per match on the held-out folds (2.5 pooled). Folds 0 and 3 go over 3 (3.8, 3.9), so the budget holds on average but not per fold.
- **The ESTIMATED-ball leak is small, and it doesn't help alarms.** The same model on `ball_source=raw`: PR-AUC 0.309 vs 0.296 held (better on 5/5 folds, +0.013 mean), ROC-AUC 0.940 vs 0.931. At the budget it's no better: 164 shots caught vs 174, 3.1 false alarms per match vs 2.5. With the raw ball the model leans harder on it (ball position 0.65 of gain vs 0.54) and less on defenders (0.06 vs 0.13): when the held ball runs out, the players carry the prediction. For the floor, the held ball is *better* on PR-AUC (0.177 vs 0.167, 5/5 folds) and worse on ROC-AUC (0.901 vs 0.918): rows without a ball get the base rate, which costs ranking but not the top of the curve. So the floor's old 0.167 wasn't inflated. Held is the number to quote from now on.
- **What the model uses** (gain share, mean over folds, H = 5):

  | group | gain share |
  |---|---|
  | ball position (dist, angle, x/y/z, in box) | 0.54 |
  | likely carrier (nearest visible attacker: goal distance, speed toward goal, distance to ball) | 0.19 |
  | defence (defenders in box, pressure, lane, keeper) | 0.13 |
  | ball motion and history (speed, toward goal, change over 1/3 s, time in final third, possession age) | 0.07 |
  | attackers in box / goal side | 0.05 |
  | visible counts | 0.02 |

  Top single features: `ball_dist` 0.46, `carrier_goal_dist` 0.11, `def_in_box` 0.08, `carrier_vgoal` 0.05, `att_in_box` 0.05, `ball_angle` 0.04. H = 3 looks the same, with `def_in_box` a bit higher (0.10).
- **For vision:** the ball's position is still half of the signal, and players are about 40% (carrier, defenders in the box, attackers in the box). The carrier is the nearest *visible* attacker, so it depends on player detection and team assignment near the ball. Short-horizon motion adds little in this form (7%), so a precise ball velocity matters less than ball position and who is near it. Gain share isn't an ablation. The sensitivity test (next on the roadmap) should measure the loss directly.
- **Early stopping lands at 86–173 rounds** (learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, min 500 rows per leaf, logloss on 15% of each fold's training matches held out). That's a small model. Parameters weren't tuned; tuning belongs inside the training matches, and it's unlikely to change the lead-time picture.
- Runtime on the M1: 6.5 min for 64 games, both horizons, 5 folds + final (features cached per match after the first run).

## What this means next
- LightGBM is the baseline the GNNs must beat: **PR-AUC 0.296 (H = 5) / 0.298 (H = 3), held ball**, with alarms at 0.849 miss rate for 2.5 false alarms per match.
- The gap to 00 is lead time, not ranking. Things worth trying, in rough order: features that see an attack *building* (how fast the carrier and ball have been approaching over 3–5 s, overloads near the box), the temporal GNN (05 model 3), and, on the evaluation side, whether 00's lead-time goal should be read at a looser budget.
- 07's open question (does a short opposing possession end an alarm) isn't what limits this model: of its 980 misses at H = 5, 941 never had p above τ in the 5 s before the shot. Only 39 had a crossing that didn't turn into a covering alarm (possession flip, dip below 0.8τ, or starting too late). Still open, since the oracle floor shows it matters once a model alarms early.
- The vision sensitivity test is next (roadmap). The importance above says to degrade the ball first, then player visibility and teams near the ball.
