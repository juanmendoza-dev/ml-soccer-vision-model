# Stage 8 on PFF tracking (2026-09-29)

`vision/state.py` (03 stage 8) run on all 64 PFF games with `python -m vision.state_check`, against PFF's possession and ball state. Inputs are what vision would see: the VISIBLE ball and VISIBLE players only.

## Bottom line
- **Possession agrees on 77.9% of frames (82.9% of alive frames). The 90% target isn't met.** Per match: 67.5% to 88.4%, median 78.3%.
- **When a carrier is confirmed, its team matches PFF 97.9% of the time** (alive frames). The rules are right when they can see. The gap is the other 74% of alive frames, where no carrier is confirmed and the last one's team is carried forward. We catch 10,074 possession changes against PFF's 14,618.
- **Ball state:** provider-dead frames come out dead or null 89.9% of the time (target 90%), but that's almost all null. Only 6.7% come out **dead**. On 4 of the games (10502, 10504, 10505, 10507), 71% of PFF's dead frames have no ball row at all (replays and cutaways), so null is right there, and where the ball is visible about half of the dead frames are called dead. Alive frames come out alive 81.0% of the time (19% null: ball unseen over 2 s).
- The "dead or null ≥ 90%" target is too easy to meet on PFF and should be read with "dead → dead" and "alive → alive" beside it.

### The change-lag rules on scored rows (2026-09-30)
Rechecking the two dropped rules on 07 #6's scored rows (H = 5): (a) `carrier_min_s = 0.1`, and (b) the new `team_near_s` field (03 stage 8, off by default) at 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 s. `PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/possession_split.py --scored h5 --state KEY=VALUE`. Rule (b) was rewritten from the description above; on the 8 dev games it lands on the old numbers (0.761 / 0.826, 0.765 / 0.827, 0.787 / 0.825, changes 2076 / 1995 / 1393 vs 2080 / 1999 / 1405).

**Bar, set before the runs.** Compared on counts of disagreeing scored rows (the rows are PFF's, so they're the same set in every config). A variant "clearly cuts" disagreement if all of these hold against the default (281,606 overall, 154,806 in the first 3 s after PFF's change, 3,862 on the positives):
- overall disagreement down at least 10% (≤ 253,445)
- first-3-s disagreement down at least 15% (≤ 131,585)
- positives not worse (≤ 3,862), and the > 10 s bucket up by no more than 1 point of rate, since the all-frames runs paid for fewer lag errors with extra flip-flops
- if more than one passes, the one with the fewest disagreeing rows overall goes to 07 #6, once. The choice is made on disagreement only, never on PR-AUC. That's the same mild use of all 64 matches as the original tuning.

- **They do go after the lag.** In the first 3 s after PFF's change the fast rules remove 14–15% of the disagreeing rows. With the ball visible the first-second rate drops from 43% to 30%.
- **They pay for it with false flips, and those land on the positives.** Disagreement more than 10 s after PFF's change goes from 4.5% to 7.5–7.7%. On the positives it roughly doubles, 7.8% to 15%, and the positives' own first-3-s rows don't improve (first second 36.5% → 36.8%). At 0.1 s, 97% of the extra positive errors are more than 3 s after PFF's change, and three quarters are more than 10 s after it. Most have the ball in view: ball visible and over 10 s after the change goes from 4.9% to 12.9%. That's an attack near the box, where a defender is nearest the ball for a moment (a block, a challenge, a ball bouncing between players) and PFF, rightly, keeps the attacking team. A shot follows those rows by definition, so they're the ones the model most needs right.
- (a) and (b) at 0.1 s are nearly the same rule (295,390 vs 295,429). The carrier rule at 0.1 s already flips on a single player's brief contact, and the team rule adds little on top.
- **0.2 s is the only one that's net better overall, by 0.9%** (−2,472 rows). It gives up most of the lag gain (−6% in the first 3 s) and still costs the positives +23%. Not near the bar.
- **Why a nearest-player rule can't win here:** how long someone has been nearest is the only thing it can tune, and one duration has to serve both cases. A real turnover needs a short one. A defender touching the ball during the other team's attack needs a long one. The two look the same to the rule, so any speed-up that follows real changes sooner also follows brief touches sooner. Even at 0.05 s, 30% of the ball-visible rows in the first second still disagree, which suggests many of PFF's changes have no visible player within 1.5 m. Not checked: it could be a ball in flight or over 1 m, a player who's on the ball but ESTIMATED (stage 8 reads VISIBLE players only), or PFF stamping the change at an event frame that doesn't line up with contact in the tracking. Either way, proximity can't catch those rows at any duration.
- So the next step is the learned rule, and the spec is written first (03 stage 8, "Learned possession (proposed)"). The positives here say what it has to do: tell a turnover from a defender's touch using the team shapes around the ball, which the nearest-player rules can't see.

- The two possession rules for dead balls made agreement worse. PFF doesn't hand possession to the restarting team when the ball goes out; it moves at its next game event.
- Even with every position, including ESTIMATED ones, alive-frame agreement stays at 83%. So on alive frames, visible vs ESTIMATED positions isn't the main gap (that run still has no ball row on about 6% of alive frames). PFF's possession comes from sparse game events (a team keeps it until its next event) and its ESTIMATED positions are poor (06: 12 m median from the shot), so there's no clean ground truth to push past this. One stretch looked at by hand (10502, period 2, a disagreement from 1610.9 s): PFF has no ball row until 1631 s, and from there away players are nearest the ball for about 5 s (ESTIMATED at first, then VISIBLE, 1.3–4 m off) while PFF says home. That's suggestive, not proof that PFF is wrong.
- The thresholds were picked on 8 games and the table below is all 64 (those 8 included). Three parameters with small effects, so little room to overfit.

## What it means
- The predictor trains on PFF's possession and would run on this one. How much that costs is 07 #6 (provider vs inferred possession, same model and folds), and that's the test that decides whether stage 8 needs more work, not the 90% line.
  - **Result (2026-09-29, `possession-inferred-2026-09-29.md`):** −0.038 PR-AUC at H = 5, 5/5 folds. It needs more work. The model collapses on the 14% of rows where stage 8 names the other team, and on those rows PFF's team goes on to shoot about 3× as often as stage 8's, so it's mostly stage 8's carried-forward team that's wrong.
- If it does: a possession change without a confirmed carrier (one-touch passes, headers, the ball arriving at a player off camera) is where the misses are. A learned rule on the last second of positions is the next thing to try, and the ball-state rules might go the same way (03 open question on throw-ins and goal kicks).
- PFF has no ball carrier, so the carrier itself isn't checked. SkillCorner has one and joins when its converter lands.
