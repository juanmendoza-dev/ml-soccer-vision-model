The documented results show a failure to distinguish early shot buildup at a useful false-alarm budget: LightGBM catches 174 of 1,154 shots, but only three with at least two seconds of warning. The leading hypotheses are objective misalignment and insufficient early discrimination; the evidence does not establish that changing to a GNN will solve either. First replay existing predictions to localize lost early warnings, then test an explicit early-window target before committing to more expensive modeling.

## Objective and base rate

- **H1 — high: the optimization objective may favor imminent-shot detection over useful advance warning.** Where: `Docs/Specs/05-prediction-model.md`, Labels, model training and Class imbalance; `Docs/Specs/07-evaluation.md:61`. Related specs: 05, 07 and `00-overview.md`, success criterion 3. A shot anywhere in the next five seconds receives the same binary target; training and early stopping use frame-level log loss, while threshold selection minimizes misses without rewarding earlier catches. Overall PR-AUC is 0.296, but early-slice PR-AUC is only 0.063 at 2–3 s and 0.057 at 3–5 s. Counterevidence: early frames are already supervised, with 28,147 positive rows 2–5 s ahead; this is not simply a shortage of early positive rows.
- **H1 suggested fix / cheap test:** Keep v1 features and LightGBM fixed and add an auxiliary target for a same-team shot in `(t+2, t+5]`, alongside the imminent-shot target. Select its threshold on inner grouped matches and measure the share of all shots warned about at least two seconds early at the same false-alarm budget. Preserve the original probability target and its calibration requirement separately. **Kill result:** no improvement in held-out early ranking or matched-budget early coverage. Higher probabilities alone are not evidence of success.

- **H2 — high: early buildup may be too ambiguous at the required base rate and alarm budget for the current inputs.** Where: `Docs/reviews/lgbm-2026-09-27.md`, Findings and H5 threshold sweep; `Docs/reviews/lead-time-2026-09-28.md`, Findings. Related specs: 05, Labels and Features; 07, Alarms and lead time; 00, success criterion 3. The positive-row base rate is 2.5%, median p two seconds before shots is 0.158, and chosen thresholds are around 0.6. At threshold 0.15, the baseline reaches a 2.09 s median but produces about 100 false alarms per match. Counterevidence: early ranking exceeds its base rate, so useful information exists; the results do not prove an information-theoretic ceiling.
- **H2 suggested fix / cheap test:** Inspect saved-score negative episodes in the score range occupied by true buildup 2–5 s before shots. Separate distinct failed attacks from repeated starts in one attack, and compare attacks with similar ball location and possession age. **Kill result:** a small, causally identifiable nuisance category or alarm-fragmentation problem explains most false positives, and removing it preserves early catches while approaching the budget. Do not treat class weighting alone as a solution: inflated scores must survive threshold reselection at the same budget.

## Labels and observation quality

- **H3 — high: possession-dependent labels may corrupt early transition sequences.** Where: `Docs/Specs/05-prediction-model.md`, Labels and Inferred possession; `Docs/Specs/07-evaluation.md`, Floor from the labels; `Docs/reviews/stage8-2026-09-29.md:23`. Related specs: 05 and 07. Only the team in possession at the prediction frame counts toward the target. PFF's sparse-event possession lag accounts for 25 oracle misses, while stage 8 agrees on only 82.9% of alive frames. Counterevidence: stage 8 itself carries stale possession forward; 97.9% agreement for confirmed carriers does not establish correctness on the remaining frames. Provider/inferred disagreement is not automatically a label error.
- **H3 suggested fix / cheap test:** Independently audit a stratified sample of shot lead-ups and matched non-shot attacks, emphasizing turnovers and possession disagreements. At −5, −3 and −2 s, inspect physical possession, provider possession, masks and target; compare early ranking on verified stable possessions versus disputed sequences. Include a small check of event timestamps against actual shot contact. **Kill result:** early labels are overwhelmingly correct and the plateau persists in verified stable possessions. The existing inferred-input experiment cannot adjudicate label truth because it retains PFF labels and alarm state. A systematic shot-clock error is unverified, not an established finding.

- **H5 — med: observation gaps may remove early cues that become visible only near the shot.** Where: `Docs/reviews/lgbm-2026-09-27.md`, Before the model and Findings; `Docs/Specs/05-prediction-model.md`, Off-camera players and Vision sensitivity test. Related specs: 05 and 07, required comparisons 4–5. Held-ball inputs leave 15.1% of scored rows without a ball, off-camera players are excluded, and the nearest visible attacker can be the wrong carrier. Documented sensitivity losses show that ball and geometry gaps matter. Counterevidence: the leaky raw-ball arm improves PR-AUC only modestly and does not improve alarms; however, its estimates are not reliable ground truth.
- **H5 suggested fix / cheap test:** Stratify saved predictions by ball freshness, available velocities and player visibility in the past window ending at each prediction time. Compare early discrimination within well-observed sequences, controlling broadly for attack geometry. **Kill result:** well-observed sequences show essentially the same plateau and no meaningful improvement with observation quality. A positive association would motivate better observations, but would not alone prove causality because camera coverage depends on the attack.

## Features and model class

- **H4 — high: aggregate features may discard useful interactions, and the proposed temporal-model interpretation overstates what is tested.** Where: `Docs/reviews/lead-time-2026-09-28.md:38` versus `Docs/Specs/05-prediction-model.md:189`. Related spec: 05, Lead-time features and Temporal GNN. The review says the temporal GNN sees per-player tracks, but the design pools each frame's graph before a GRU and explicitly has no node links across time. It tests sequences of spatial configurations, not explicit identity-linked runs. v2's 21 summaries took 24% of gain without improving early ranking; that rejects those summaries, not temporal information generally. Counterevidence: many plausible summaries already failed, so additional history alone is insufficient justification.
- **H4 suggested fix / cheap test:** Correct the interpretation in a future documentation change. Compare ordered lags of existing features with window summaries using the same small model family. In queued GNN work, compare hybrid frame GNN against globals-only, then temporal against frame GNN; include a history-order shuffle using only already-observed frames. **Kill result:** ordered history adds nothing over summaries or shuffled history, and graph interactions add nothing over globals at matched budgets. This would weaken the tested representations, but cannot falsify identity-linked trajectories that the architecture does not consume.

- **H6 — med: fixed LightGBM regularization may hide rare early patterns and confound the v2 feature conclusion.** Where: `Docs/reviews/lgbm-2026-09-27.md:31`; `Docs/reviews/lead-time-2026-09-28.md:34`. Related specs: 05, Baseline; 07, Splits. The model uses 31 leaves, minimum 500 rows per leaf, feature subsampling and log-loss early stopping. Moving from 29 to 50 features without retuning tests the feature set under one configuration. Counterevidence: v2 loses PR-AUC on every fold, stopping rounds are similar, and early probabilities barely move; a large tuning rescue is not supported by current evidence.
- **H6 suggested fix / cheap test:** Run a small, predefined inner-grouped comparison of capacity and regularization, especially minimum leaf size and feature subsampling, on cached v1/v2 features. Track early discrimination as well as log loss. **Kill result:** flexibility improves training fit or late-shot ranking but leaves held-out early discrimination unchanged. This rejects simple underfitting as the main explanation, not every possible advantage of a neural model.

## Evaluation and interpretation

- **H7 — med: evaluation choices can obscure progress or consume the alarm budget, but are unlikely to explain the plateau alone.** Where: `Docs/Specs/07-evaluation.md`, Alarms and lead time and Floor from the labels; `Docs/reviews/lead-time-2026-09-28.md:22`. Related specs: 07 and 00, success criterion 3. Lead medians compare different caught-shot sets; threshold choice rewards total catches; alarms run through set plays excluded from scored training rows. v2's inner-selected threshold yields 4.56 false alarms per match outside training versus the intended maximum of three. Quantile calibration deciles do not resolve calibration at the extreme tail where alarms start. Counterevidence: 941 of 980 baseline misses never cross threshold in the preceding five seconds, matched-budget sweeps still plateau, and the label oracle achieves 4.9 s median lead under these rules.
- **H7 suggested fix / cheap test:** Replay existing scores to separate no early crossing, early crossing lost before coverage, and missing/unscorable lead-up. Compare leads on the same caught shots and report ≥2 s coverage over all shots. Attribute false alarms to set-play contexts, possession changes and repeated starts, and inspect tail calibration and per-match budget spread. **Kill result:** early crossings remain rare and plausible alarm-policy variations barely improve matched-budget coverage. In that case, evaluation remains a reporting/deployment concern rather than the principal cause.

## Test order and safeguards

- **First: H7, using existing predictions.** It is the cheapest way to locate where potential warnings disappear and check whether training is the appropriate next intervention. Existing evidence predicts that lack of early crossings will dominate.
- **First retraining experiment: H1.** An explicit early-window target directly tests objective alignment while holding features and model class fixed. This is more diagnostic than immediately increasing architectural complexity.
- Audit H3 alongside these diagnostics before interpreting provider/inferred differences as model failures. Use H5 strata to decide whether additional representation work is limited by observations.
- All tuning, feature selection, calibration and threshold selection belong inside grouped training matches under spec 07. Pooled out-of-fold threshold sweeps are descriptive, not deployable threshold selection. Existing outer folds have already informed development; do not choose variants by repeatedly optimizing their reported scores. Leave the IDSSE final test untouched.
- Keep missed-shot rate, actual false alarms per match and the fraction of all shots with ≥2 s warning beside conditional median lead. A better median on fewer caught shots is not sufficient progress toward a useful warning system.
